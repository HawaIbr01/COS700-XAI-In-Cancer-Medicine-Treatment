\subsection{Pipeline Overview}
\subsubsection{ANN: Baseline Training and Overfitting Diagnosis}
Person 1 is responsible for training the baseline neural network on the preprocessed GDSC dataset and establishing the "Overfitting 
Baseline" – the starting point of the RED XAI diagnostic pipeline. This section details the model selection rationale, training 
configuration, and the specific criteria used to confirm that the baseline model exhibits the high Generalisation Gap required for 
Stage 1 of the experimental design.

\subsubsection{Model Selection: DeepCCDS as the Shared Platform}
DeepCCDS (Wu et al., 2025) is adopted as the shared neural network platform for the reasons detailed in Section 3.4.1. Critically for 
the experimental design, the same architecture can be trained under two distinct optimisation regimes – standard Adam without 
regularisation (Stage 1 Baseline) and SAM (Stage 2 Intervention) – allowing for a controlled comparison that isolates the effect of 
loss landscape geometry on explanation quality. The group's contribution is not the construction of a novel architecture from scratch. 
Rather, it is everything built around the network: the evolutionary rule extraction algorithms (Persons 2 and 3), the SAM regularisation
intervention (Person 4), and the multi-criteria evaluation framework (Person 5). Person 1's role is to establish the controlled baseline 
and ensure a standardised model checkpoint is shared with all team members. 

\subsubsection{Dataset and Preprocessing Interface}
The GDSC dataset, as preprocessed following the Alipour (2024) pipeline, provides the training data. The preprocessing steps – merging of 
the three GDSC sub-datasets (cell line features, drug information, and drug response measurements), removal of duplicates, handling of 
missing values, and encoding of categorical features – produce a structured tabular dataset where each row represents a cell line–drug 
combination characterised by gene expression features and a continuous drug sensitivity target (LN IC$_{50}$).
Prior to model training, the following additional preprocessing steps are applied to ensure compatibility with DeepCCDS and the 
downstream rule extraction pipeline:
The continuous gene expression features are Z-score normalised across all samples, such that each gene's expression is expressed 
relative to the population mean and standard deviation. This is consistent with the biological discretisation described in 
Section 4.1, which categorises features into High Expression (Z > 1), Low Expression (Z < -1), and Intermediate Expression for the 
symbolic rule extraction stage. The train/validation/test split is fixed at 80\%/10\%/10\% and determined prior to any model training. 
This split is documented as a shared interface specification and used by all team members (Persons 1, 2, 3, and 5) without modification, 
ensuring that rule quality metrics are computed on identical held-out data throughout both stages of the experiment.

\subsubsection{Stage 1: Training the Overfitting Baseline}
The baseline model is trained using the Adam optimiser with a learning rate of 0.001 and no explicit regularisation (no weight decay, 
no dropout). To allow clear overfitting to emerge, training continues until the validation loss has not improved for a fixed number of 
consecutive epochs, a standard early stopping criterion applied in reverse, where the patience parameter is used to confirm that the 
generalisation gap has stabilised rather than to halt training prematurely (Goodfellow et al., 2016). The stopping point is defined as the epoch at which training loss continues to decrease while
validation loss has plateaued or begun to increase for at least \textcolor{red}{10} consecutive
epochs \textcolor{red}{(a provisional value, subject to confirmation during pilot testing)},
producing a measurable and stable generalisation gap.
The Generalisation Gap is formally defined as:
\\
$\text{Generalisation Gap} = \text{Validation Loss} - \text{Training Loss}$
\\
The magnitude of the gap is recorded in terms of both R² and RMSE on the regression target (LN IC$_{50}$) and constitutes the confirmed 
Fragility Baseline. This mirrors the diagnostic framing established by Pahde et al. (2023), where a model achieving high training 
performance through spurious correlations serves as the starting condition for the bias identification and correction cycle. 
\textcolor{red}{As no single generalisation-gap threshold is established in the pharmacogenomic
  literature, the baseline gap is interpreted relative to the held-out performance reported for the
  regularised DeepCCDS model (Wu et al., 2025): the unregularised baseline is expected to achieve
  strong training performance while underperforming this published reference on the test set,
  thereby confirming overfitting.}
The trained baseline model weights are saved as a checkpoint and shared with Persons 2, 3, and 5. Persons 2 and 3 load this 
checkpoint and do not retrain, ensuring that their evolutionary rule extraction algorithms operate on exactly the same model in 
both Stage 1 and Stage 2.

\subsubsection{Confirming the Clever Hans Baseline}
In addition to the generalisation gap metric, a qualitative sanity check is applied, inspired by the approach of Pahde et al. 
(2023): feature attribution maps are generated for a sample of training and validation predictions using a basic post-hoc attribution 
method such as input gradients or a lightweight LRP implementation. If the baseline model is genuinely overfitting to training noise, 
attribution maps for correctly predicted training samples will exhibit diffuse or biologically incoherent relevance patterns, assigning 
high importance to genes with no established role in the relevant oncogenic pathway, while validation attribution patterns will be 
inconsistent across samples. This qualitative check documents the fragility of the baseline model's explanations and motivates the 
subsequent SAM intervention. The generalisation gap measurement and attribution patterns together constitute the quantitative and 
qualitative inputs to the Stage 1 audit, formally establishing the Fragility Baseline scorecard against which Stage 2 results are compared.
