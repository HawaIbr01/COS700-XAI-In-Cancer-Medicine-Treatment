\label{sub:method_nsga}
The Non-Dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II) is implemented as a 
comparative multi-objective rule extraction technique alongside the single-objective 
GA pipeline. This generates the evaluative structure needed to determine which 
evolutionary paradigm produces more resilient rules under conditions of high model 
fragility. The implementation leverages the identical DEAP framework and operates 
on the identical discretised GDSC dataset split and saved deep learning model checkpoints 
used in the GA workflow, ensuring absolute experimental control across both rule 
extraction strategies.
\\
Unlike the single-objective GA variant which combines competing evaluation goals into 
a scalar composite fitness function, NSGA-II segregates individual criteria and 
optimises them simultaneously. The algorithm maximises fidelity and accuracy, 
measured via the macro F1 score of the extracted rules against the predictive outputs 
of the underlying black-box DeepCCDS model, while simultaneously minimising rule 
length, quantified by the total number of antecedent conditions per rule, to suppress 
rule explosion and guarantee structural comprehensibility \cite{Yedjour2024}.
\\
Selection leverages a binary tournament operator based on the crowded comparison 
approach. When comparing two distinct candidate rule sets, the algorithm prefers the 
individual with the lower non-domination rank; where both candidates reside on the 
same Pareto front, it selects the rule set exhibiting the larger crowding distance to 
preserve genetic diversity across the objective space. 
\\
Two-point crossover and random mutation operators are applied identically to the GA 
sub-module to maintain rigorous comparative boundaries \cite{Yedjour2024}. The 
NSGA-II loop runs for a matching duration of 50 generations with an identical population 
size. Unlike the GA, the final output maps a complete Pareto front of non-dominated 
rule strings, which is passed directly to the PROMETHEE II framework in Section 
\ref{ssub:method_promethee} for Stage 1 and Stage 2 comparative evaluation.
