\label{ssub:critical-analysis}

The literature reviewed in Section~\ref{sec:literature} reveals the existence of a diagnostic gap at
the intersection of model robustness and explanation quality.
\\
Section~\ref{ssub:xai} established that XAI exists in two cultures. Blue XAI has dominated the field in
terms of adoption, producing methods that justify model outputs to end users. Red XAI, as formalised
by Biecek and Samek~\cite{Biecek2024}, reorients XAI as an active interrogation tool, one that
questions whether a model deserves to be explained at all before any explanation is surfaced. The Red
XAI philosophy is well-articulated in theory; what remains is a complete pipeline that implements this
philosophy from overfitting diagnosis through to measurable improvement in symbolic explanation
quality. This project fills that gap.
\\
The GDSC dataset, while invaluable as a pharmacogenomic resource, exhibits the $P \gg N$ structure
that reliably induces overfitting in unregularised deep models \cite{Partin2023}. Models must process
tens of thousands of gene expression features measured across a comparatively small number of unique
cancer cell lines, even though these are tested against hundreds of drugs to form a large dataset of
combinations \cite{Wu2025}. Klauschen et al.~\cite{Klauschen2024} and Partin et al.~\cite{Partin2023}
demonstrate that clinicians cannot verify whether a model's predictions are grounded in genuine
oncogenic biology or learned shortcuts without an explicit auditing mechanism. The literature in
cancer AI has produced increasingly powerful predictive models but has not systematically addressed
the question of whether those models are ready to be explained, that is, whether a model has
generalised well enough that an explanation will reflect genuine biological signal rather than
memorised noise, as distinct from merely attaching a post-hoc explainability method to a model whose
internal logic has never been audited \cite{Wicker2023,Ye2024}. The
distinction matters clinically: a biologically incoherent rule extracted from an overfitted model may
appear statistically valid while pointing clinicians toward irrelevant or even harmful treatment
decisions \cite{Pahde2023,Klauschen2024}.
\\
Section~\ref{ssub:sam} reviewed the theoretical relationship between loss landscape geometry and
generalisation, establishing that models converging to sharp minima exhibit brittle, poorly
generalising representations \cite{Li2018,Keskar2016}. SAM was reviewed as an intervention that
explicitly seeks flat minima during training \cite{Springer2024}. What the literature has not
established, however, is whether the geometric improvement that SAM produces in the loss landscape
translates into measurable improvement in the quality of symbolic rules extracted from the model. The
connection between landscape geometry and explanation quality has been theorised but not empirically
demonstrated in the pharmacogenomic context. This project provides that empirical test.
\\
Section~\ref{ssub:ann} reviewed the ANN literature relevant to this project, covering the DeepCCDS
architecture and the Clever Hans problem as documented by Pahde et al.~\cite{Pahde2023}. The R2R
framework represents the closest methodological precedent: it uses XAI to identify model artefacts,
localises them, and corrects model behaviour iteratively. However, R2R operates on image data, uses
LRP-based attribution heatmaps as its explanation medium, and corrects models through fine-tuning with
artefact masks. It does not address the tabular genomic domain, does not use symbolic rule extraction
as its explanation modality, and does not investigate whether regularisation applied at the
optimisation stage can prevent the Clever Hans problem from arising rather than repairing it after the
fact. This project is therefore differentiated from R2R in domain, explanation type, intervention
mechanism, and causal direction.
\\
Section~\ref{ssub:ea} reviewed the evolutionary algorithm literature, specifically GAs and NSGA-II as
applied to rule extraction. The GPRO\_RX system demonstrated that multi-objective genetic algorithms
guided by PROMETHEE-assisted ranking can produce rule sets that outperform single-objective extraction
methods on the combined criteria of fidelity and comprehensibility \cite{Yedjour2024}. However,
GPRO\_RX and the broader EA rule extraction literature evaluate their methods on models with no
deliberate control over the model's generalisation state; rule extraction is applied to whatever model
happens to have been trained, without investigating whether the model's level of overfitting mediates
rule quality. The comparative value of GA versus NSGA-II for rule extraction from models at different
points on the generalisation spectrum remains unexplored. This project introduces that control by
extracting rules from both a deliberately overfitted baseline and a SAM-regularised model, isolating
the contribution of model health to rule quality independently of the extraction algorithm used.
\\
Section~\ref{ssub:auditing} reviewed the quality framework for symbolic explanations, fidelity,
comprehensibility, and stability as defined by Arrieta et al.~\cite{Arrieta2020} and operationalised
through PROMETHEE II ranking by Yedjour et al.~\cite{Yedjour2024}. Adebayo et al.~\cite{Adebayo2018}
showed through sanity checks that popular XAI methods can produce visually convincing explanations
even for models that have memorised noise. To the best of current knowledge, the literature does not
establish a quantitative relationship between the size of the generalisation gap and the degradation
of rule quality metrics. Whether a smaller generalisation gap is a statistically reliable predictor of
higher fidelity and lower rule complexity, the core hypothesis of this project, has not been tested.
Section~\ref{ssub:gsea} further noted that even statistically valid rules require biological
verification through pathway enrichment analysis to confirm clinical relevance, introducing a layer of
grounding that purely metric-based XAI evaluation cannot provide.
\\
Taken together, the literature has established four things independently: that overfitting produces
unreliable explanations \cite{Pahde2023,Adebayo2018}; that loss landscape geometry is a measurable
indicator of generalisation quality \cite{Li2018}; that SAM can improve landscape geometry
\cite{Foret2021}; and that evolutionary algorithms can extract high-quality symbolic rules from neural
networks when properly configured \cite{Yedjour2024}. The contribution of this project is not any
single component: the ANN baseline, the SAM optimiser, the GA, the NSGA-II, and the PROMETHEE ranking
are all individually established. The contribution is the controlled pipeline that connects them, the
experimental structure that isolates model health as the independent variable, and the empirical
evidence it generates about the relationship between generalisation and explainability in the
pharmacogenomic domain.
\\
While Yedjour et al.~\cite{Yedjour2024} demonstrated that PROMETHEE-assisted multi-objective genetic
algorithm rule extraction outperforms single-objective methods, their evaluation was conducted
exclusively on the UCI benchmark datasets and was not tested on high-dimensional genomic data, where
the curse of dimensionality and the presence of batch effects present substantially different
challenges to rule search spaces.
\\
Springer et al.~\cite{Springer2024} established that SAM produces more balanced feature representations
than conventional regularisers, but their analysis was limited to image classification benchmarks and
does not provide evidence of this effect on tabular pharmacogenomic data or on the downstream quality
of symbolic rule extraction.
\\
Pahde et al.~\cite{Pahde2023}, while providing a compelling audit-and-fix framework through Reveal to
Revise, operate exclusively in the image classification domain using LRP-based attribution heatmaps.
Their correction mechanism is applied post hoc through fine-tuning with artefact masks, whereas this
project intervenes at the optimisation stage to prevent Clever Hans behaviour from arising rather than
correcting it after the fact.
\\
Li et al.~\cite{Li2018} provide strong theoretical and empirical evidence connecting flat minima to
improved generalisation, but do not investigate how loss landscape geometry affects the quality of
downstream symbolic explanations, leaving the connection between landscape smoothness and explanation
fidelity as an empirically untested claim that this project is designed to address.
\\
Adebayo et al.~\cite{Adebayo2018} demonstrated through sanity checks that popular attribution methods
can produce convincing explanations for models that have memorised noise, but their study did not
evaluate symbolic rule-based explanation methods or propose a regularisation intervention as a
corrective mechanism, leaving the question of how to prevent rather than detect this behaviour
unresolved.
\\
A closely related body of work, and arguably the nearest methodological neighbour to this project, is
explanation-guided training: methods that modify the training objective itself to improve the
robustness or correctness of model explanations. This cluster includes Right for the Right Reasons
\cite{Ross2017}, contextual-decomposition penalisation \cite{Rieger2020}, adversarial training on
explanations \cite{Tang2022}, robust-explanation objectives such as R2ET \cite{Chen2022} and REGEX
\cite{Li2023}, and the Clever-Hans-specific pruning method EGEM \cite{Linhardt2024}. Two distinctions
separate this project from that cluster. First, what enters the loss: these methods place the
explanation itself into the optimisation objective, in some cases requiring human-annotated
ground-truth explanations \cite{Ross2017} or expensive second-order gradients, whereas SAM is
explanation-agnostic and flattens only the loss landscape, so any gain in rule quality is an emergent
byproduct of model health rather than a directly optimised target. This yields a cleaner scientific
question, namely whether geometric regularisation alone, with no explanation in the training loop,
improves explanations. Second, the explanation medium and domain: the explanation-guided cluster
operates on continuous saliency or attribution maps in image and text classification, whereas this
project produces global symbolic rules in pharmacogenomics with biological verification through GSEA.
No prior work is known to test whether explanation-agnostic geometric regularisation improves the
quality of global symbolic rules in this domain.

\subsubsection{Contributions}
\label{ssub:contributions}

This project makes the following concrete contributions to the literature. The first is
methodological. It introduces and evaluates a Red XAI pipeline that uses the generalisation gap as a
diagnostic gatekeeper for symbolic explanation quality in cancer pharmacogenomics, a combination of
domain, explanation type, and diagnostic mechanism that, to the best of current knowledge, has not
been previously investigated.
\\
The second is empirical. It provides a controlled experimental test of whether SAM regularisation, by
smoothing the loss landscape, produces a statistically significant improvement in the fidelity and
comprehensibility of evolutionary rule extraction on GDSC drug sensitivity data. Statistical rigour is
ensured through Mann--Whitney U testing of the PROMETHEE II net outranking flows of the baseline and
SAM-regularised rule sets, which constitute two independent samples (Section~\ref{sec:methodology}).
To account for the stochasticity of evolutionary rule extraction, extraction is repeated across 30
independent runs and the resulting non-dominated rules are aggregated before ranking.
\\
The third is evaluative. It provides a comparative assessment of two evolutionary rule extraction
approaches (single-objective GA and multi-objective NSGA-II) applied to both a baseline overfitted
model and a SAM-regularised model, across a consistent evaluation framework informed by Arrieta et
al.~\cite{Arrieta2020} and ranked through PROMETHEE II. This comparative structure generates evidence
about which extraction method is more resilient to model fragility, a question that has not been
addressed in the existing EA-XAI literature.
\\
The fourth is diagnostic. The loss landscape visualisation produced for both the baseline and
SAM-regularised model constitutes a novel proposed indicator of explainability readiness, a geometric
measure of whether a model is positioned to yield reliable explanations before any extraction is
attempted. This moves the XAI quality question upstream, from the explanation evaluation stage to the
model training stage, consistent with the Red XAI philosophy of interrogating the model rather than
justifying it.
