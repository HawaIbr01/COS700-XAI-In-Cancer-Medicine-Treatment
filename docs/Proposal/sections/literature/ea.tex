\label{ssub:ea}

Evolutionary algorithms (EAs) are a class of stochastic search and metaheuristic optimisation
techniques inspired by the biological processes of natural evolution and selection. Unlike traditional
algorithms that iterate on a single solution, EAs maintain a population of individuals, where each
individual represents a potential solution to a problem \cite{Yedjour2024}. The population evolves over
generations using three primary operators: selection, crossover, and mutation.
\\
Rule extraction is a post-hoc explainability technique used to identify the underlying decision-making
logic of opaque black-box models such as ANNs \cite{Yedjour2024,Ghasemi2024}. It is fundamentally a
multi-objective optimisation problem requiring a simultaneous balance between fidelity to the
black-box model, predictive accuracy on unseen data, and rule comprehensibility \cite{Yedjour2024}.
Deterministic rule extraction methods such as the CART decision tree \cite{Breiman1984} impose fixed
inductive biases on the search, for example, axis-aligned splits that limit depth and greedy coverage
strategies, which
constrain their ability to navigate the combinatorial complexity of high-dimensional genomic feature
spaces or to optimise competing objectives simultaneously \cite{Arrieta2020,Yedjour2024}. EAs are
well suited to this problem precisely because they maintain a diverse population of candidate rule
sets, can be configured with flexible multi-objective fitness functions that reflect fidelity,
accuracy, and comprehensibility simultaneously, and are capable of escaping local optima through
mutation and crossover operators \cite{Yedjour2024}. Among EA variants, Genetic Algorithms and NSGA-II
are selected for this project: GAs provide a single-objective baseline for rule extraction quality,
while NSGA-II explicitly evolves a Pareto front of non-dominated rule sets (those for which no
objective can be improved without worsening another), enabling a structured
trade-off analysis between fidelity and comprehensibility that single-objective methods cannot produce
\cite{Yedjour2024}.
