\subsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

This study treated explanation as an interrogation of the network, as Red XAI proposes \citep{biecek2024}. In this design, the generalisation gap, which SAM is designed to reduce, did not track explanation quality. SAM+WD cut the gap by about 86\%, mostly by raising the training loss, and its validation RMSE worsened (1.072 against 1.030). Fidelity, length and stability stayed essentially flat across the three networks. For explainable prediction in cancer medicine, the network's behaviour therefore has to be interrogated directly, through its extracted rules and attributions, and not inferred from a training metric. Earlier work has also found post hoc explanations to be fragile or unreliable \citep{ghorbani2019,slack2020}.

The small fidelity gain was largely a weight-decay effect. The combined effect of SAM+WD against Adam looked like a SAM benefit, but it was entirely weight decay for PDERE-GA. For PDERE-NSGA the clean SAM contrast was $+0.0057$ (not significant), against $+0.0076$ for weight decay. Any claim that a training method improves explanations therefore needs a control for what else changed with it. Training also changed what the network attended to even though its predictions barely moved. SAM+WD concentrated its attribution slightly more (top-10 share 43\% against 35\%), but 6 of its 10 top pathways were shared with the Adam network, and Adam+WD was not audited, so this cannot be credited to SAM. It does support auditing explanations whenever training changes.

The extracted rules are not ready to stand in for the network in a clinical setting. Fidelity was moderate on a harsh scale (median 0.43 to 0.47), the genes used changed from fold to fold, and known drug--target links were almost never recovered. A mutation flag that marks any recorded variant cannot be read as an activating mutation, which limits any automatic reading of a rule. The significant fidelity effects (0.007 to 0.014 on a scale from 0 to 1) are detectable across 379 drugs but would not change a treatment decision.

The research questions can be answered as follows. RQ1 was not tested, because three networks cannot support a correlation: the gap fell from 0.337 to 0.273 to 0.048 while the rule measures stayed flat, which is a description and not a correlation. For RQ2, there was no clean SAM effect on fold-to-fold stability for either extractor, and the only significant result was the combined effect (SAM+WD against Adam) for PDERE-NSGA. For RQ3, SAM+WD outranked the Adam baseline on $\varphi$, but weight decay alone explained the PDERE-GA result, and the clean SAM effect for PDERE-NSGA was not significant after correction. RQ4 was only partly tested: the gene-use pattern and positive controls suggest weak alignment with known drug targets, but the KEGG test was uninformative. The hypothesis expected SAM to give simpler, more faithful and more stable rules. SAM+WD gave slightly higher fidelity than Adam, but weight decay largely accounts for it, and the one SAM-specific difference that survived correction went the opposite way (slightly longer PDERE-NSGA rules, by about 0.1 conditions). In this design, no evidence was found that SAM improves rule quality beyond what weight decay provides.
